**2. Competitor pages**
- **Akbar Travels** [1] (title = H1). H2s: Srinagar, Gulmarg, Pahalgam, Sonamarg, wazwan, best time, FAQ. Long. FAQ: famous food, national dish, 36-course meal, cost, vegetarian, breakfast, two flight questions. Gaps: one-line dish notes; calls rogan josh the national dish.
- **TravelTriangle** [2] (H1: Famous Kashmir Food and Kashmiri Dishes That Are Must Try In 2026). One H3 per dish, then FAQ. Long; updated 2025, titled 2026. FAQ: wazwan dish count, Jammu food, top dishes, kahwa, vegetarian. Gaps: includes momos, thukpa, butter tea, khambir, thenthuk; no prices.
- **Holidify** [3] (H1: 19 Kashmiri Food Dishes That You Must Try (Zaeka-e-Kashmir)). One H2 per dish. Long, undated, no FAQ. Gaps: 9 of 19 dishes are Ladakhi/Tibetan; no breads, street food, harissa, places or prices.

**3. Questions**
- What is the national dish of Kashmir? / What is the 36-course meal in Kashmir? / What do Kashmiris eat for breakfast? (Akbar Travels FAQ)
- What is the famous food of Jammu? / Which are some popular vegetarian Kashmiri dishes? (TravelTriangle FAQ)
- What is the famous food of Kashmir? (Quora [4])

**4. Verified facts**
1. Rice is the staple, and most Kashmiri meals include no bread [5].
2. Muslims cook with onion, garlic and shallots where Pandits use asafoetida [5]. Sources disagree on meat: Wikipedia says Pandits prefer lamb, JKTDC says young goat [5][6].
3. Haakh (collard greens) features in most meals [7].
4. JKTDC and Wikipedia put the royal wazwan at 36 courses, 15–30 of them meat [6][8]; a 2015 Tribune report describes wedding wazwan as at least 15 dishes [9]. Treat 36 as an ideal, not a fixed count.
5. Diners share a copper traem in fours; seven dishes are essential, and gushtaba ends the main course [10].

**5. Verdict**
Informational/travel intent. Competition: moderate; strong OTA domains lead with shallow, error-prone pages. WazwanWay can compete with a meal-by-meal, season-aware guide linking its dish pages. Overlap: moderate with /kashmiri-food; high with /restaurants/best-wazwan-srinagar, titled "Best Kashmiri Food in Srinagar". Best page type: guide.

**3. Questions**
- Where can one get the best street food in Srinagar? (Quora [14])
- What is the most popular street food in Kashmir? (Quora [15])
No fetched page had an FAQ; Reddit is blocked for the tool.

**4. Verified facts (including places)**
1. Khayam Chowk: kebab street where a cinema shut amid militant violence in the early 1990s; one kebab shop became over a dozen eateries; busiest at Ramzan iftar [16].
2. Hazratbal shrine bazaar: masal tchot, halwa with a 3-ft poori, fried fish, nadir monji; busiest on Fridays and religious anniversaries [17][18].
3. Masal tchot (lavasa, boiled chickpeas, radish-yogurt chutney) [19] sells until about 1–2 pm outside shrines, colleges and courts, e.g. Makhdoom Sahib, Eidgah [20].
4. Winter harissa: downtown kadals, e.g. Aali Kadal, Nawakadal [21], Fateh Kadal [22].
5. Lal Chowk appears only on travel-publisher pages [1][23]; no news source found.
6. In Feb 2025 a dead rodent in frying oil at a Hazratbal stall led to a police case; officials cited over Rs 42 lakh in food-safety fines the year before [24].

**5. Verdict**
Informational/travel intent, with local-listing intent for Srinagar. Competition: low–moderate (estimate); thin listicles, no place-by-place guide. WazwanWay can win with a "where and when" guide plus hygiene tips. Overlap: moderate. The street-food hub names no places, and the masala-tsot, nadur-monji, mutton-tujji and seekh-kebab dish pages should be linked, not repeated. Best page type: blog.

**3. Questions**
- Which is the best Wazwan Restaurant in Srinagar? (Quora [28])
- Is food expensive in Kashmir? (Akbar Travels FAQ [1])
No tour page had an FAQ; Tripadvisor blocked fetching.

**4. Verified facts**
1. Ahdoo's opened in 1918 as a Residency Road bakery and was third-generation owned in 2018 [29].
2. Teams of six to eight wazas from downtown Srinagar cook wedding wazwan; in 2015 the season ran mid-April to October [9]. "Wazapora" as the waza quarter appears only on YouTube and blogs.
3. JKTDC frames the Timur/Samarkand origin as the wazas' own claim [6].
4. Diners sit in fours at a traem after ritual hand-washing [10].
5. Timing matters: harissa is breakfast-only, October–March [21]; Hazratbal peaks Fridays [17]; Khayam Chowk at iftar [16].
6. Outlook Traveller (Jan 2024) cost for two: Shamyana ₹800, Mughal Darbar ₹1,000, Ahdoos ₹1,500 [18]. Recheck.

**5. Verdict**
Commercial/travel intent (tours, Tripadvisor, YouTube); the wazwan variants are local. Competition: moderate. Articles are weak, but WazwanWay sells no tours, so aim for the informational share with a self-guided Srinagar food walk. Overlap: high with /how-to-experience (~400 words, est.), best-wazwan-srinagar, restaurant-vs-wedding-vs-home and the food-trail itinerary. Best page type: guide; answer the wazwan variants by expanding /how-to-experience.

**2. Competitor pages**
- **Kashmir Despatch** [30] (title = H1). H2s: al hachi, ruwangan hachi, hokhegad, handh, gogji aar, farrigad, murabba, harissa. Medium, Nov 2024, no FAQ. Gaps: little on cooking or buying; unsourced medicinal claims.
- **Hamiast** [31] (title = H1; numbered list, no H2s). Ten items mixing clothing (pheran, kangri) with foods. Medium, 2023, no FAQ; sells products. Gaps: a 1–2-hour harissa method, not the shops' overnight cook.
- **GOYA** [32] (H1: Eating Harissa in Old Srinagar; no H2s). Medium, 2021, no FAQ. Gaps: unnamed shop, 2021 prices, unsourced Mughal origin.

**3. Questions**
- How to make 'Harissa', the famous dish in Kashmir in the winter? (Quora [33])
- What is hokh syun? (Slurrp headline [34])
- Are sun-dried vegetables safe for consumption? (Greater Kashmir headline [35])
- What is Chillai Kalan? (India.com headline [36])

**4. Verified facts**
1. Harissa season: October–March, served 7–10 am [21]; one shop opens 4 am, December–February [32]. WazwanWay's dish page says November–March.
2. It's a breakfast dish with methi (minced intestines), kebab, hot oil and kander czot, cooked overnight in a firewood-heated mathh [21].
3. Origin is tradition: Shah-i-Hamdan per a maker [21], the Mughals per GOYA [32], a Persian link per Wikipedia [37]. Shop ages of 150–350 years are unverified [21][22][38].
4. Hokh syun: dried tomato, fenugreek, lotus stem, bottle gourd, turnip, spinach [5], and fish [39]. Drying months conflict: Aug–Sep [39], Apr–Oct [40], May–Jun [41].
5. Declining with better roads, greenhouses and milder winters [40]; dried gourd cost ₹600/kg in Srinagar in Dec 2024 [42].
6. Unnamed oncologists quoted by Greater Kashmir found no cancer link; advice: moderation, check for fungus [35].
7. Chillai Kalan: 21 Dec to 29 Jan (Wikipedia [43]) or 31 Jan (Kashmir Life [39]).

**5. Verdict**
Informational, plus recipe and local intent for harissa. Competition: low for the head term (local news, retailer blogs); moderate for harissa (PARI, GOYA). A "Kashmir's winter table" blog can rank with a Chillai Kalan calendar and a hokh syun table. Overlap: the harissa dish page (~1,800 words, est.) keeps "harissa srinagar"; the winter itinerary lacks food links. Best page type: blog.
