# SERP and competitor research: Cuisine and culture clusters

_Collected 14 September 2026 with a web search tool that returns US results (not Google India). No search volumes, keyword difficulty or ranking data were available; nothing here is an estimate of volume._

## Cluster: kashmiri cuisine

**1. SERP sample** (US results)
"kashmiri cuisine":
1. en.wikipedia.org — Wikipedia — Kashmiri cuisine
2. en.wikipedia.org — Wikipedia — List of Kashmiri dishes
3. patelbros.com — food site — Taste Of Kashmir…
4. mydiversekitchen.com — personal blog — Kashmiri Cuisine Through The Ages…
5. holidify.com — travel publisher — 19 Kashmiri Food Dishes…
6. reenamoza.com — personal blog — Kashmiri Recipes…
7. khyberhotels.com — hotel site — Top 10 Must-Try Kashmiri Foods…
8. awesomecuisine.substack.com — personal blog — A Food Lover's Guide…

The variant "traditional kashmiri food" adds two travel publishers: flamingotravels.co.in (#1) and intermiles.com.

**2. Competitor pages**
- **Holidify**
  - H1: "19 Kashmiri Food Dishes That You Must Try"; one H2 per dish.
  - Medium length; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: nine of the 19 dishes are Ladakhi or Tibetan (momos, thukpa). It doesn't separate Pandit and Muslim cooking, and gives no places to eat or seasons.
- **Patel Brothers** (2024)
  - H2s: rogan josh, yakhni, dum aloo, pulao, phirni, kahwa.
  - Medium; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: only six dishes. It calls dum aloo tomato-based, but Wikipedia says the Kashmiri version is yogurt-based.
- **The Khyber**
  - The title says "Top 10" but the H1 says "Top 5". H2s: kahwa, harissa, Wazwan dishes, tujj, trout.
  - Medium; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: promotes the resort. It treats Wazwan as all Kashmiri food and never mentions asafoetida or dry ginger.

**3. Questions people ask**
- Quora:
  - What is the famous food of Kashmir?
  - What are the food habits of the people of Kashmir?
  - Which spices are popular in Kashmiri cuisine?
  - What is the difference between Kashmiri Pandit food and normal North Indian/Himalayan food?
- Question-style search: Is Kashmiri food spicy?

**4. Verified facts**
1. Wikipedia splits the cuisine into Pandit and Muslim styles. The methods are similar, but Muslims use onion, garlic and shallots where Pandits use asafoetida and curd. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_cuisine))
2. A chef gives the Pandit base as fennel powder, dry ginger powder, turmeric and asafoetida, with no onion, garlic or tomato ([Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/experiences/food-and-drink/celebrating-kashmiri-pandit-cuisine-with-phira-saal)). A Pandit writer adds mustard oil and Kashmiri chilli ([Daily Excelsior, 2026](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/kashmiri-cuisine-a-heritage-beyond-the-plate/)).
3. Kashmiri red chilli is mild (1,000–2,000 SHU). The Spices Board says it grows mainly in J&K and Himachal, and also in Goa, so the name doesn't prove it came from Kashmir. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_red_chilli))
4. Pandit rogan josh gets its colour from ratanjot (alkanet root) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_cuisine)). The Muslim version uses praan (Kashmiri shallot) and cockscomb flower, called mawal ([List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kashmiri_dishes)).
5. Sahapedia says praan is what separates wazwan from Mughlai cooking. It gives a figure of 7–10 kg of praan paste per 100 kg of food, without a source. ([Sahapedia](http://www.sahapedia.org/wazwan-culinary-legacy))
6. Rice is the staple. The Kashmiri equivalent of "bread and butter" is haakh-batte, meaning greens and rice. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_cuisine))

**5. Verdict**
- **Intent:** informational, with some travel "must-try" interest.
- **Competition: moderate.** Wikipedia holds #1–2, and the rest are listicles with errors.
- **Angle:** a sourced pillar page explaining both kitchens (Pandit and Muslim) and everyday versus feast food, linking to the 47 dish pages.
- **Overlap:** moderate with the ver masala, red chili and fennel/dry ginger posts.
- **Best type:** guide or pillar page.

## Cluster: kashmiri pandit food

**1. SERP sample** (US results; few results; there is no Wikipedia article on this)
"kashmiri pandit food":
1. namratawakhloo.medium.com — personal blog — Kaeshur Khyen: Cuisine of Kashmiri Pandits
2. outlooktraveller.com — news/magazine — Beyond Wazwan: …'Phira Saal'
3. indianholiday.com — travel publisher — Kashmiri Pandit Cuisine
4. hamiast.com — food retailer blog — Kaeshur Khyen (same article)
5. thespiceangel.com — food site — Kashmiri Pandit Cuisine
6. manjulikapramod.com — personal blog — Kashmiri Pandit Cuisine – PENDOWN

The variant "kashmiri pandit cuisine" adds cultureandheritage.org (blog) and paannyaar.com (food site).

**2. Competitor pages**
- **Kaeshur Khyen** (Namrata Wakhloo; Hamiast copy, 2024)
  - About 22 H2s, one per dish (roganjosh, kaliya, kabargah, haakh, gaad…).
  - Long; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: the most detail on dishes, but nothing on festival or wedding food or meat customs, and no sources.
- **Outlook Traveller** (Dec 2023)
  - H2s: "Phira Saal for Kashmiri Pandits"; "Pan-India Platters".
  - Medium; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: a write-up of a hotel food festival. It dates the tradition to 726 BCE in the subhead and 326 BCE in the chef's quote, with no source for either.
- **cultureandheritage.org** (2024)
  - H2s: heritage, ingredients, dishes, techniques, festivals.
  - Medium; no FAQ.
  - Inaccuracy: calls the cuisine "predominantly vegetarian" and suggests a paneer or potato rogan josh. It sidelines the mutton and fish tradition.

**3. Questions people ask**
- Quora:
  - Do Kashmir Pandits eat meat?
  - Are all Kashmiri Pandits vegetarians?
  - Why do Kashmiri Pandits eat non-vegetarian food?
  - Do Kashmiri Brahmins eat beef?
  - What is the difference between Kashmiri Pandit food and normal North Indian/Himalayan food?
- The "kashmiri pandit wedding food" results are all wedding-planning sites.

**4. Verified facts**
1. Pandits traditionally eat mutton and fish, but not beef or pork. Wikipedia cites Witzel (1991) and Simoons (1994). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_Pandits))
2. A Pandit writer says chicken, eggs, onion and garlic were traditionally left out, and elders even avoided carrots and tomatoes. Practice varies between families. ([Daily Excelsior, 2026](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/kashmiri-cuisine-a-heritage-beyond-the-plate/))
3. At Herath (the Pandit Shivratri), soaked walnuts are shared as a religious offering ([Daily Excelsior](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/herath-the-kashmiri-pandits-sacred-mahashivratri/)). Families cook meat, fish and cheese, though some families outside Kashmir now avoid meat at Herath ([ETV Bharat, 2025](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!state/kashmiri-pandits-celebrate-herath-with-cultural-and-religious-fervour-enn25022603371)).
4. At Gaad Batt, fish and rice are offered to the household guardian deity, usually in December. ([Free Press Journal, 2025](https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/netflixs-baramulla-shows-unique-kashmiri-pandit-ritual-know-more-about-gaad-batt))
5. At Khetsimavas (the new moon of Pausha, Dec–Jan), khichri is offered to Kubera and the Yakshas. The main source is a Wikipedia page flagged "needs more citations", so present it as tradition. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_Hindu_festivals))
6. Wedding cooking begins with vaer, a savoury rice porridge ([List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kashmiri_dishes); [eSamskriti](https://www.esamskriti.com/e/Culture/Ceremonies/Kashmiri-Pandit-Wedding-~-KAESHUR-KHANDAR---1.aspx)). Sources disagree on meat at weddings:
   - [WeddingWire India](https://www.weddingwire.in/wedding-tips/kashmiri-pandit--c1449) says it was traditionally forbidden.
   - Outlook Traveller says the Phira Saal platter served to the groom after the wedding includes meat dishes.
7. Wikipedia's dum aloo page calls it a Pandit dish but lists garlic, which contradicts the sources above. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dum_aloo))

**5. Verdict**
- **Intent:** informational and cultural.
- **Competition: low to moderate.** There is no Wikipedia article, and blogs and retailers contradict each other on whether Pandits are vegetarian.
- **Angle:** a neutral, sourced explainer could rank, ideally reviewed by a Pandit contributor. It should cover meat customs, onion and garlic, festival foods and differences between families.
- **Overlap:** low, except with /guide/kashmiri-wedding-food on weddings.
- **Best type:** long-form blog.

## Cluster: kashmiri wazwan

**1. SERP sample** (US results; wazwanway.com does not appear)

1. asiamediacentre.org.nz — news/magazine — The intricacies of Kashmiri Wazwan
2. budgam.nic.in — government — Wazwan
3. en.wikipedia.org — Wikipedia — Wazwan
4. deccanherald.com — news/magazine — A wazwan to remember…
5. hamiast.com — food retailer blog — Kashmiri Wazwan…
6. linsfood.com — food/recipe site — Kashmiri Cuisine, Wazwan…
7. wanderlog.com — travel publisher — kashmiri wazwan
8. atlasobscura.com — news/magazine — This Kashmiri Feast Features 36 Dishes

The variant "wazwan meaning" adds Wiktionary, sahapedia.org (cultural archive), ethnq.com (blog) and definitions.net.

**2. Competitor pages**
- **Wikipedia**
  - H2s: History; List of main dishes.
  - Medium; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: the word's meaning, the 36-course claim and the serving customs have no citations. There is no evidence on origins. The Budgam district government page copies its wording.
- **Asia Media Centre** (May 2024)
  - Sections: influences, restaurateur interview, praan, preparation, etiquette, order of dishes.
  - Medium; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: claims "up to 72 dishes" and gives the Timur origin with a hedge. Nothing on cost, where to eat or vegetarian options.
- **Sahapedia** (Raja Muzaffar Bhat)
  - Sections: signature dishes, praan, the wazas of Wazpur.
  - Long; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: its "600 years from Central Asia" origin has no source. Nothing on etiquette or cost.

**3. Questions people ask**
- Quora:
  - What is Wazwan in Kashmir?
  - What does a typical Kashmiri Wazwan meal consist of?
  - Which is the best Wazwan Restaurant in Srinagar?
  - What is Kashmiri Wajvan? (misspelling)

**4. Verified facts**
1. Waz means cook and wan means shop ([Budgam district](https://budgam.nic.in/culinary-delight/wazwan/); also on Wikipedia, uncited).
2. The number of dishes varies by source:
   - A peer-reviewed review says 7–36 ([Rather et al., 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S235261811630124X)).
   - Wikipedia says 36 courses, 15–30 of them meat, uncited.
   - Asia Media Centre says up to 72.

   Treat "36" as an ideal, not a fixed rule.
3. Timur, camped on the Indus in 1398, demanded tribute from Sultan Sikandar of Kashmir, and the two never met ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikandar_Shah_Miri)). Wikipedia's Kashmiri cuisine page speaks of an "invasion of Kashmir", so sources conflict. Treat the story of Timur's Samarkand cooks as legend.
4. Sikandar did host scholars and Sufis from Isfahan, Bukhara and Balkh (same page). A 2026 [Kashmir Times](https://kashmirtimes.com/opinion/comment-articles/kashmirs-grand-wazwan-was-saved-by-wazas-from-gurez) opinion piece notes there is no direct archaeological evidence that wazwan began in the Valley.
5. The vasta waza (spelled "wouste waze" on Wikipedia) is the master chef who supervises the overnight cooking ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazwan)). He oversees 15–20 cooking pots, called degs ([Asia Media Centre](https://www.asiamediacentre.org.nz/features/the-intricacies-of-kashmiri-wazwan)).
6. Srinagar's Wazpur district has been home to wazas for generations (Sahapedia). Waza families in Gurez trace their roots to Rainawari and Ali Kadal in Srinagar and, by family memory, moved to Gurez around 1886 (Kashmir Times).
7. Four people share each trami, the shared platter, and wash hands at a basin called the tash-t-naer. The seven essential dishes are tabakh maaz, rista, rogan josh, daniwal korma, aab gosh, marchhwangan korma and gushtaba. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazwan))

**5. Verdict**
- **Intent:** informational, with some local "where to eat" searches.
- **Competition: moderate to high.** Wikipedia, a government page and magazines hold the top spots.
- **Overlap: high.** WazwanWay already has 19 guides, including four dish or menu pages, and two history pages that duplicate each other (the complete-history-of-wazwan post and the history-of-wazwan guide). A new blog would compete with the site's own pages.
- **Recommendation:** update /guide/what-is-wazwan as the main page instead. Add a sourced "myths vs evidence" box (36 courses, Timur), a table of dish counts and an FAQ, and merge the duplicate history pages.
- **Best type:** update the existing guide.

**1. SERP sample** (US results)

1. vegrecipesofindia.com — food/recipe site — Collection of Tasty Vegetarian Kashmiri Recipes

**2. Competitor pages**
- **Incredible India**
  - Title: "10 Best Vegetarian Dishes To Try In Srinagar"; ten H2s, one per dish (haak, nadru yakhni, lyodur tschaman, al yakhni, dum olav, rajma gogji…).
  - Long; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: accurate, and credits Pandit cooking. No Wazwan context, places to eat, seasons or date.
- **Backpackers United** (2024)
  - H1: "A Vegetarian's Guide to Kashmiri Cuisine…"; eight dish H2s.
  - Medium; no FAQ.
  - Gaps: no restaurants or seasons; ends with a tour-package pitch.
- **kashmirirecipe.com** (2024)
  - 15 dish H2s.
  - Long; has an FAQ.
  - Gaps: recipe-focused, with nothing for travellers.

**3. Questions people ask**
- Quora:
  - What is the best vegetarian food to explore when in Kashmir?
  - Can I get vegetarian food in Kashmir?
- Page FAQ (kashmirirecipe.com):
  - Is Kashmiri vegetarian food very spicy?
  - Which is the most famous vegetarian Kashmiri dish?
  - What's the main spice used in Kashmiri vegetarian cuisine?

**4. Verified facts**
1. Wikipedia says Wazwan dishes are almost all meat. It names dum aloo, nadur (lotus stem) and haakh as the vegetarian options, and its dish list has only ruwangan chaman and dum aelve. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazwan))
2. Wikipedia's List of Kashmiri dishes keeps a "Wazwan dishes" section apart from vegetarian sides, paneer dishes (tsaaman) and dals. This is the clearest split between feast and everyday food I found. ([List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kashmiri_dishes))
3. Incredible India credits the vegetarian dishes to Pandit cooking and never mentions Wazwan. It describes rajma gogji as made without onion, tomato or garlic. ([Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/jammu-and-kashmir/srinagar/10-best-vegetarian-dishes-to-try))
4. Haak rus is whole collard leaves cooked with water, oil, salt, green chillies and spices. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakh))
5. The nadru (lotus stem) harvest starts in September ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_cuisine)). In 2025, lotus grew back in Wular Lake about 30 years after silt from the 1992 floods buried it ([PTI/Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/j-k/lotus-beds-reappear-in-jammu-and-kashmirs-wular-lake-after-3-decades/)).
6. Wikipedia calls dum aloo a Pandit dish with a yogurt or khoya sauce, but also lists garlic, which is inconsistent. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dum_aloo))
7. Wikipedia calls ruwangan tsaman (tomato and paneer) a vegetarian Wazwan dish ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruwangan_tsaman)). It describes rajma gogji (kidney beans with turnip) as a winter staple ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razma_gogji)). Both articles are short.

**5. Verdict**
- **Intent:** informational plus travel.
- **Competition: low to moderate.** One government page, then thin travel-agency listicles. None separates home dishes from Wazwan or tells vegetarians where to find them.
- **Angle:** a practical "vegetarian's guide to eating in Kashmir" could compete.
- **Overlap: high** with /guide/vegetarian-wazwan, the nadru post and the dish pages. Frame the piece around everyday and travel eating, and link to those pages rather than repeat them.
- **Best type:** travel-led blog.
