# A. SEO research summary: WazwanWay blog expansion

_Prepared 14 September 2026. Research came first; the strategy in `B-content-calendar.md` is built on it._

## How the research was done, and its limits

- **Keyword discovery.**
  - Ran 1,036 Google autocomplete queries (India and US markets) and 35 Bing autosuggest queries (India).
  - Seeds were the brief's keywords plus question, comparison, price, recipe and location modifiers.
  - This returned 2,414 unique suggestions. After removing noise such as "Srinagar, Garhwal" or "Kashmiri Gate", 2,203 were relevant.
- **Interest proxy.** Used English Wikipedia pageviews from September 2025 to August 2026. This is a real, public signal of topic interest, but it is not Google search volume.
- **SERP and competitor analysis.**
  - Covered 16 keyword clusters, sampling the top results with a web search tool that returns US results. Google India will differ.
  - Read the 2–3 leading pages in each cluster for structure, depth, FAQs, gaps and errors.
- **Fact-finding.**
  - Collected 5–8 sourced facts per cluster from Wikipedia, government sites (JKTDC, District Budgam, Incredible India), Kashmiri media (Kashmir Life, Greater Kashmir, Kashmir Monitor) and national media and archives (PARI, Sahapedia, Outlook).
  - Recorded conflicts between sources rather than resolving them silently.
- **Site inventory.** Recorded the title, H1, headings and approximate depth of every page among the 141 sitemap URLs, and read the key overlapping guides in full.
- **Not available.** Google Search Console, GA4, Keyword Planner and paid SEO tools (Ahrefs, Semrush, DataForSEO).

> **There are no search volumes or keyword-difficulty scores anywhere in this document.** Demand and competition are qualitative assessments, labelled as such. Connect Search Console before publishing so real impression data can replace them.

Full research notes, with every source link, are in `research/serp-travel.md`, `research/serp-cuisine.md`, `research/serp-commercial.md` and `research/serp-dishes.md`.

## Competitor observations

- **Travel publishers dominate discovery searches with shallow, error-prone lists.**
  - Holidify's "19 Kashmiri food dishes" includes nine Ladakhi or Tibetan dishes (momos, thukpa and others).
  - TravelTriangle's page lists momos, thukpa, butter tea, khambir and thenthuk; it carries a 2026 title but was last updated in 2025.
  - Akbar Travels calls rogan josh "the national dish".
  - Patel Brothers describes Kashmiri dum aloo as tomato-based.
  - Almost none separate Wazwan from everyday Kashmiri food, name places, give seasons or date their information.
- **Wikipedia holds the head terms ("kashmiri cuisine", "wazwan"),** but many of its key claims are uncited, including the 36 courses, the meaning of the word and the Timur origin story. Its own pages also disagree: one says Pandits prefer lamb, while JKTDC says young goat.
- **Kashmiri media and cultural archives hold the best on-the-ground detail.** Kashmir Life, Greater Kashmir, Kashmir Monitor, PARI and Sahapedia publish news features rather than structured guides. They are the best sources and the weakest SEO competitors.
- **Recipe results are saturated.** For "noon chai recipe" and "kahwa recipe", 13 of the 13 recipe pages checked had Recipe schema, 8 had star ratings and 5 had video.
- **Restaurant results are local listings plus unsupported "best" lists.** None of five independent lists gave evidence for its picks. The three restaurants that appeared across all five were Ahdoos, Mughal Darbar and Shamyana.
- **Shopping results are vendor-owned.** Authenticity advice such as home saffron tests and "Mamra" almond origin claims appears only on seller pages.
- **Snippet and People Also Ask opportunities.** These tools couldn't extract People Also Ask boxes. Question-style queries seen in autocomplete, on Quora and in on-page FAQs include:
  - "What is the famous food of Kashmir?"
  - "What do Kashmiris eat for breakfast?"
  - "Do Kashmiri Pandits eat meat?"
  - "Is jain food available in kashmir?"
  - "What is kong phirin?"
  - "Is Kashmiri food spicy?"

  Every draft opens with a direct 40–70-word answer and ends with an FAQ built from these questions.

## Content gaps WazwanWay can fill

1. **Everyday Kashmiri food vs Wazwan.** Readers are told that Wazwan *is* Kashmiri food. Explaining the difference is accurate and differentiating.
2. **Time and place.** Masala tsot in the morning, Khayam Chowk's grills in the evening, Hazratbal on Fridays, harissa at dawn in winter. No competitor organises food this way.
3. **Seasons.** No food guide covers the harissa season, hokh syun (sun-dried vegetables), the September lotus-stem harvest, Ramadan-only phirni or Chillai Kalan.
4. **Kashmiri Pandit cooking with neutral sourcing.** Blogs disagree on whether Pandits eat meat, and there is no Wikipedia article.
5. **Practical help for vegetarian and Jain travellers.** No page separates home dishes from Wazwan or explains how to order.
6. **A neutral food-shopping guide.** None covers the Kashmir saffron GI tag, the IIKSTC lab in Pampore, walnut shell types, or the fact that India imports far more almonds than Kashmir grows.
7. **Myth-busting.** The "36 courses" is an ideal, not a rule; Timur never entered Kashmir; momos aren't Kashmiri; the restaurant "Kashmiri pulao" isn't the Valley's everyday rice; red doesn't mean hot.

**Other structural issues**
- `/kashmiri-food/wazwan`, `/bakery`, `/beverages` and `/street-food` serve identical server-rendered H1s and headings ("The definitive guide to real Kashmiri food"), so they are unlikely to rank for their own titles.
- `/restaurants/best-wazwan-srinagar` is titled "Best Kashmiri Food in Srinagar" and already targets "best wazwan in srinagar" and "best food in srinagar".

**The 10 new blogs therefore target adjacent demand that the site doesn't yet own, and link into the Wazwan guides instead of competing with them.**

**Existing claims to review.** The research turned up a few statements on the site that should be checked, because the new articles will link to these pages:
- `what-is-wazwan` presents the story of Timur's Samarkand cooks as fact. Timur never entered Kashmir, and Wikipedia and JKTDC treat the story as tradition.
- The cost guide gives undated prices, and its wedding season (August–November) conflicts with other pages and with The Tribune (mid-April to October in 2015).
- `/dishes/kashmiri-pulao` makes royal-kitchen claims without sources.

## Recommended strategy

1. **Hub and spokes.**
   - `/blog/kashmiri-cuisine-explained` is the pillar: what Kashmiri cuisine *is*.
   - `/blog/what-to-eat-in-kashmir` is the traveller hub: what to *eat*.
   - Eight spokes cover narrower intents. Each spoke links to the pillar and hub and to 3–5 relevant existing pages; the pillar and hub link back out.
2. **Win on accuracy and usefulness.** Give dates, sources, seasons, places and practical tips. Label tradition as tradition, and correct the common errors competitors repeat.
3. **Answer first.** Open each article with a short direct answer (a featured-snippet target), then give depth, then an FAQ.
4. **Upgrade existing pages instead of adding more Wazwan posts.**
   - Make `what-is-wazwan` the Wazwan pillar, with a "myths vs evidence" box and a dish-count table.
   - Merge the thin duplicate dish-list and history guides into their strongest page, with 301 redirects.
   - Add dated, sourced facts and vegetarian options to `/restaurants/best-wazwan-srinagar`.
   - Expand `/how-to-experience` into a self-guided Srinagar food walk.
   - Add a dated "thali vs trami" section to the cost guide.
   - Improve the `noon-chai` and `kashmiri-kahwa` dish pages (tested quantities, video, a "why isn't it pink?" fix) for recipe searches.
   - Give each `/kashmiri-food/*` category page its own server-rendered H1 and introduction.
5. **Fix the template before publishing** (details in `README.md`):
   - The blog renderer doesn't display Markdown tables (`remark-gfm` isn't installed).
   - There's no FAQPage schema.
   - Every post shares one Open Graph image.
   - Posts have no reviewer or author credentials.
   - New posts need entries in `data/relatedReading.js` and, for long titles, in `SEARCH_TITLES`.
6. **E-E-A-T.**
   - Have a Kashmiri Pandit cook or community member review the Pandit food article, and credit the review if they agree.
   - Use owned photography, and keep the "fact-check sources" lists in each draft for editors.
7. **Measure.**
   - Connect Search Console and submit each post when it goes live.
   - Track impressions and average position per primary keyword.
   - Review after 6–8 weeks, and refresh the winter and shopping posts before their seasons.
