# Sources for the core seven stories

_All read on 15 October 2026 unless noted. Each entry says what the source supports for these seven dishes and what to be careful of. Sources already in the site's registries (`frontend/data/dishNotes.js`, `wazwanPillar.js`) keep their ids._

## Reference works and dictionaries

- **grierson** — G. A. Grierson, *A Dictionary of the Kashmiri Language*, Digital South Asia Library (dsal.uchicago.edu). Entries checked: *rōgan*, p. 828: "grease, varnish, polish; clarified butter, ghī". *dāñĕwal*, p. 232: greens of the coriander plant; *-gūjü* powdered coriander seed; *-kulu* the coriander shrub. *maʦh* 3, p. 609: "a soup containing balls (or cakes) of this pounded meat fried in oil". *yĕkhañĕ*, p. 1192: "a kind of dish (consisting of meat cooked with spices in curdled milk)". *dŏd*, p. 190: milk. Already in the site: *tabakh* p. 962 (a large dish, plate or tray), *māz* p. 615 (meat), *marʦ* p. 594 (red chilli; *-wã̄gan-āra*, "a chain or string of red chillis, dried and kept for winter use"). The site returns excerpts only; no entry for *josh* or *āb* came up.
- **wp-wazwan** — Wikipedia, "Wazwan". "Seven dishes are a must for these occasions", cited to *The Hindu* (11 April 2012) and The Manor Delhi's menu: tabakh maaz, rista, rogan josh, daniwal korma, aab gosh, marchhwangan korma, gushtaba. The traem's contents (two seekh kababs, four methi maaz, two tabakh maaz, safed and zafran kokur); tash-t-naer; "wouste waze"; gushtaba ends the main course (cited to *Deccan Chronicle*). Uncited: 36 courses with 15 to 30 of meat; waz = cook, wan = shop. Dish lines: tabak maaz twice-cooked ribs; rista "red, paprika-saffron-fennel spice gravy" coloured with alkanet; aab gosh fennel, cardamom, partially evaporated milk; daniwal korma "lamb roasted with yoghurt, spices and onion puree, topped with coriander leaves"; marchwangan korma "chicken legs/thighs cooked in a spicy browned-onion sauce" (the list of dishes says "extremely spicy lamb dish"); gushtaba "velvety textured meatball in white yogurt gravy". No Timur story on this page.
- **wp-rogan-josh** — Wikipedia, "Rogan josh". Etymology (Collingham 2006, Delahunty 2008, Chapman 2009, Ayto 2012, Wahhab 2016; OED for pronunciation); Persian origin and Mughal route (Collingham; the "brought by the Mughals in the 16th century" sentence is uncited); ingredients (Panjabi 1995: garlic, ginger, clove, bay, cardamom, cinnamon; some versions onion or yoghurt; praan and maval for colour and a "cooling" effect; yoghurt and asafoetida for body); *dampokhtak* finish (Singh 1973); alkanet (Wahhab); Kashmiri chilli (Majumdar; Brahmin versions "preclude the use of onions, ginger and garlic"); British restaurant tomatoes and red peppers (Dillon, BBC); Jaffrey's paprika-to-cayenne ratio (1982); Kapoor's restaurant version (2011); Holkar 1975 "meat and tomatoes"; Bhangal 2013 Punjabi tomato version; Owen 1994 beef brisket; Collingham on foreign visitors' first Indian meals. Full reference list recorded in `01-rogan-josh.md`.
- **wp-kashmiri-cuisine** — Wikipedia, "Kashmiri cuisine". Names of rogan josh, yakhaen, ab gosht, riste and goshtabh "stem from Persian sources" (cites Rather et al. 2016); korma "has Turkish roots"; Pandits use asafoetida, Muslims onions, garlic and praan (Roy 2016; Kaul Kilam); Muslims prefer goat, Pandits lamb or sheep (Roy; JKTDC says young goat, so note the disagreement); mawal boiled to make red colouring used mostly in Wazwan (uncited); Pandit cuisine uses ratanjot for colour in rogan josh (uncited); Kashmiri chilli "in excess" for colour and tomato wrongly substituted, "No authentic Kashmir-cooked version uses tomatoes" (cites a 2018 blog recipe "Rogan Josh: Ghulam Waza's Kashmiri Mutton Curry"); saffron extract colours rogan josh and rista (Chef Jolly); rista and goshtabeh poached in lamb stock with cinnamon and black cardamom, meat folded while beaten (Chef Jolly); slaughtered fresh and pounded before rigor mortis, rarely found outside the valley (*The Hindu* 2018); chef Nazir Ahmed Aram on copper content of pots (fn 138, *National Geographic Traveller*); walnut and apple wood preferred (uncited); charcoal gives best results (Daily Excelsior); mushk budji rice GI in February 2022.
- **wp-list** — Wikipedia, "List of Kashmiri dishes". Rogan josh "seasoned with liberal amounts of Kashmiri chillies" (refs 72 to 74: *Dawn* 2014, *Independent* 2022, Vir Sanghvi); tabakhmaaz "rib racks in ghee" (Hamiast, a retailer), kabargah the Pandit name, boiled with salt and garlic, kabargah simmered longer and fried quickly (Daily Excelsior; HydFoodGuy; Gastro Obscura); aab gosh "milk-based curry cooked in spices and ghee over a low flame", ten litres of cow's milk reduced to one, also called dodhe maaz (refs 37, 38: LBB Delhi; *The Kashmir Walla*); danival kormeh "lamb cooked with coriander or parsley" (uncited); martswangan kormeh "meat cooked with spices and yogurt and mostly using Kashmiri red chillies and hot in taste" (uncited; the chicken version cited to a Delhi menu); yakhean without turmeric or chilli (NDTV); goshtabeh "minced mutton balls with spices in yogurt gravy" (Times of India recipes); methi maaz "mutton intestines" with dried fenugreek.
- **wp-goshtaab** — Wikipedia, "Goshtaab" (Gushtaba redirects here). "King of Kashmiri wazwan"; final course (Razdan 2004; NDTV; *The Hindu* 2012); pounded "for hours" (*Deccan Chronicle*); "believed to have been introduced" under the Mughals (Sen 2015; *The Wire* 2018, which in fact says nothing about origins). No GI mention.
- **wp-riste** — Wikipedia, "Riste (dish)". Short: mutton, beef, rarely chicken; ground meat with spices and eggs, boiled half an hour then cooked in gravy (NDTV recipe); Razdan 2004 p. 45.
- **wp-tabak-maaz** — Wikipedia, "Tabak maaz". Ribs "simmered in milk and spices and then fried" (uncited); also called qabargah (Daily Excelsior). Its references include a *Journal of Ethnic Foods* paper, "Bhat (2015)", whose DOI resolves to a paper on an Ethiopian beverage, and a "Waza (2017), HarperCollins" entry that does not match the Roli book: treat the page's references as unreliable.
- **wp-aab-gosh** — Wikipedia, "Aab gosh". A stub: lamb in a milk-based gravy, part of Wazwan. Its refs include Rising Kashmir's recipe (below) and *The Kashmir Walla*.
- **wp-daniwal** — Wikipedia, "Daniwal korme". A stub: mutton in a yogurt gravy with fresh coriander; "daniwal" means coriander (Times Now 2025); "korme" means braise (Merriam-Webster).
- **wp-kashmiri-chilli, wp-alkanet, wp-tomato, wp-korma, wp-abgoosht** — already in the site's registry; used as before (1,000 to 2,000 SHU and the CERC tests; alkanet's dye dissolves in oil; tomato's post-European arrival; korma from *qormā*, braising; the Persian abgoosht).
- **rather-2016** — Rather, Masoodi and Akhter, "Ethnic meat products of Kashmiri wazwan: a review", *Journal of Ethnic Foods* 3(4), 2016, doi 10.1016/j.jef.2016.12.002. Abstract read via Semantic Scholar: a wazwan has 7 to 36 dishes; the key ethnic meat products are kabab, tabak maaz, aab gosh, rogan josh, nate-yakhni, rista and goshtaba; "traditional knowledge is poorly documented". The full text is open access on ScienceDirect but could not be fetched. Cite the abstract only.

## Cookbooks

- **roli** — Sharief Waza, Waza Shafi and Waza Rafiq with Rocky Mohan, *Wazwaan: Traditional Kashmiri Cuisine*, Roli Books. The publisher's listing (via Powell's and ACC Art Books): "Seven dishes typically form an indispensable part of the feast: tabakh maaz, rista, Rogan Josh, dhaniwal korma, Aab gosh, marchwagan korma and ghustaba"; 96 pages; "the first book detailing the secret recipes from the renowned family of Waza". Only the tabakh maaz recipe has been read, in the Scroll excerpt below.
- **scroll-tabakh-maaz** — Scroll.in, 14 February 2020, excerpt from the Roli book: ribs boiled in water, skimmed, garlic and salt, boiled until the membrane can be pierced with a thumb; cut into eight rectangles; boiled again with turmeric, dry ginger, cloves, black cardamom; fried in 475 g pure ghee until reddish brown; the ghee drained before serving. The headnote contrasts the Muslim wazwaan with the Pandits' *butta* and says both favour lamb and wood-fire cooking.
- **kaul-kilam** — S. S. Kaul Kilam and Shyam Rani Kilam, *Culinary Art of Kashmir* (De Leij), first published 1988 per Wikipedia's list; online at ikashmir.net (© 2005 Kashmir News Network). The site asks that recipes not be reproduced, so the articles describe, not copy. Recipe 40, Neni Rogan Josh: 1 kg mutton, mustard oil, curd, cloves, salt, sugar, red chilli powder, turmeric, dry ginger, "aniseed" powder, cumin, garam masala, a teaspoon of ghee, a pinch of asafoetida; no onion, garlic or ratan jot; curd-coated meat fried in mustard oil until golden brown, chilli, turmeric and sugar with a little water until brownish-red, then water with dry ginger and aniseed, simmered "until the meat is tender and the gravy is red, thick, and oily"; ghee and garam masala at the end. Recipe 44, Kabargah Ta Tabaq Maz: ribs in 2 × 4 inch pieces with fat and skin on, simmered in water with half a teacup of milk and the dry spices, dipped in a curd batter and deep-fried in ghee (kabargah), or sealed between ghee-smeared clay plates, *tabaqs*, with dough and cooked over slow charcoal for about an hour (tabaq maz); "generally served as a last Meat Course by Kashmiri Pandits". Recipe 190, minced meat for goshtaba and rista: boneless leg, "preferably freshly slaughtered", about 250 g suet, a wooden mallet (*goshpar*) on a flat hard stone, suet pounded in, then ginger, aniseed, black cardamom, garam masala, salt and arrowroot; no egg. Recipe 205, Goashtaba: "The King of Dishes" or "Dish of Kings", "the last Meat Dish in a 'Vazavan' Feast, before the Dessert"; balls 2 to 3 inches; simmered with salt, aniseed and ginger powder; the strained broth cooked with curd, cloves and garlic, with fried shallot and onion, finished with garam masala, cinnamon, cardamoms and mint. Note: this goashtaba recipe uses garlic, shallot and onion, so the book is not uniformly in the no-onion Pandit manner; describe recipes individually.

## Reporting and features

- **sahapedia** — Raja Muzaffar Bhat, "Wazwan: A Culinary Legacy", Sahapedia (undated). Rogan josh: 70 to 80 g pieces from the lower ribs, lower back (*bukhtor*), chest and neck, simmered in a gravy of garlic, ginger, red chilli powder, clove, cardamom, cinnamon and praan paste, served hot with rice. Aab gosht: tail, shoulder or shank, boiled, cooked with shallot, garlic, ginger powder, fennel powder, cloves, cardamoms, cinnamon; full-cream dry milk; ghee to finish; on the trami with rice. Tabak maaz: fatty ribs simmered with spices, cut, fried in ghee until crisp and caramelised; a starter. Rista 80 to 100 g, one per diner. Gushtaba: meat minced after slaughter with a wooden hammer on a flat stone, with fresh sheep fat and black cardamom; boiled; yakhni of yogurt and mutton broth with shallots, ginger, garlic, cinnamon and cardamom, topped with cumin and dry mint; 200 to 400 g, serves four, the eldest portions it; the final course. Wazas: 3 to 4 with assistants for 200 to 300 kg; *daigs*; fruit-tree wood (apple, walnut, plum; acacia and willow occasionally); 18 to 20 hour days; named wazas Fayaz Ahmad (Wazpur), Jahangir Ahmad (29), Ghulam Hassan (60), Farooq Ahmad; pay figures. Presents the 600-year Central Asian origin and the seven-to-12-24 growth as fact.
- **asia-media-centre** — Anusha Kulal, "The intricacies of Kashmiri Wazwan", Asia Media Centre, 29 May 2024. Azmat Ali Mir (Sarposh, Bangalore): "When it comes to serving the various dishes, the order is set in stone"; praan "the life of Wazwan"; ghee for an authentic Wazwan, mustard oil at home. Vasta waza over 15 to 20 degs; apprentices chop, portion and pound; wood from fruit trees; mawal gives the deep red; order tabak maaz, seekh kebab, methi maaz, then rista, rogan josh, daniphol, aab gosht, gushtaba last; up to 72 dishes at a wedding, at least seven at a funeral.
- **outlook-diplomacy** — Naseer Ganai, Outlook India, 27 April 2022. Order: seekh kababs, tabakh maaz, daeni phoul and chicken first; methi, rista, rogan josh, dhaniwal korma, marchhwangan korma; aab gosht; yakhni "the finishing dish served in wazwan before gushtaba"; gushtaba last. Dish lines: rogan josh "red lamb curry"; dhaniwal korma "mutton cooked in a lot of coriander"; marchhwangan korma "a fiery hot mutton cooked with Kashmiri red chilli"; aab gosht "sheep ribs cooked in a milk-based gravy and flavoured with green cardamoms and saffron". Nickel-plated copper *daegs*; 10 to 36 meat dishes cooked overnight under the vasta waza; "no set rules" on which dishes are served; origin theories (Zain-ul-Abidin, contested; Saleem Beg on caravans); Sadiq in the 1960s.
- **kashmir-walla** — Zenaira Bakhsh, "The origin, evolution of Kashmir's emblematic cuisine Wazwan", *The Kashmir Walla*, 15 July 2021. Mohammad Shafi Khan, second-generation waza of the Ahad family, downtown Srinagar: about twenty pieces of mutton per utensil for roganjosh; "Ten litres of pure cow milk are reduced to one and then mixed with the mutton" (aab gosht); "If the mutton is left idle and minced later, one can't make goshtab"; goshtaba "the showstopper". Rogan josh: medium chunks from the lower back, slow-cooked; red from red chillies and saffron ground in water and cockscomb flower extracts. Marchwangan korma: "smaller chunks of meat than used in roganjosh and cooked in a thick chilli gravy". Tabakhmaaz: whole ribs boiled with salt and garlic, chopped, fried crisp. Rista: small meatballs in mutton stock with cardamom, cloves, garlic and Kashmiri chillies, first dish served, four per traem. Goshtaba: one large meatball cut into four, cooked in yoghurt, ghee and stock with dry mint; final dish. Daniwal korma kokar (chicken). Prof Mohammad Ashraf Wani (former history professor, Kashmir University): dishes of wazwan introduced with saints from Iran and Central Asia; modified until Kashmir's own. Shams-ud-din Ahmad (former Head of Persian, Kashmir University), *Shah-i-Hamdan aur Kashmir*: Persian chefs taught Kashmiris kabab, roganjosh, korma, yakhni, pulao, aabgosh, kofta and rista; four-to-a-plate eating adapted from Central Asian and Iranian villages. Over 200 kg chopped and pounded through the night; cooking from dawn for about six hours; copper *tasht*, *naer*, *traem*, *sarposh*.
- **gulf-news-2022** — Sadiq Shaban, Gulf News, 20 January 2022. The *vur*, an open kitchen; firewood "logs and birch branches"; meat pounded "with Kashmiri walnut wood hammers on stone"; copper degs simmering "under an autumn moon"; wazas serve "exactly four pieces of a course" to four spots on the trami. Rogan josh "shoulder cuts cooked in flower essence, curd, and Kashmiri chilly"; daniwal korma "mutton cooked in coriander"; "Marchwagun Korma (fiery hot Kashmiri chilly korma)"; goshtaba "a huge ball of meat, marinated with blobs of golden fat", "a yardstick to measure the chef's culinary skills", "a full stop"; yekhni "towards the close". Order as listed. Kehwa from a samovar after.
- **gulf-news-goshtaba** — Abdul Rashid Waza, "Classic Kashmiri Goshtaba recipe from a waza", Gulf News, 20 January 2022; Gulf News calls him "a Dubai-based Kashmiri homecook". Home recipe: minced leg of lamb with egg whites, fat and mustard oil; a spice pouch (ginger, fennel, cinnamon, cardamoms) boiled with bay leaves; balls simmered an hour; yogurt and milk finish; dried mint listed; saffron. Useful as a home version, not as the wazas' method.
- **reuters-2026** — Reuters, "Photos: Kashmir's 600-year-old Wazwan wedding feast adapts to modern life", Al Jazeera, 30 September 2026 (photos Sharafat Ali). "The rhythmic thud of wooden mallets pounding lamb"; wazas "pound meat with wooden mallets on stone slabs to prepare gushtaba and rista"; *degchis* over a wood fire; rogan josh "a slow-cooked lamb curry and signature dish"; gushtaba the final dish; Ghulam Nabi Baba, 74: "There were only seven mutton dishes at that time. Over the centuries we added more, and now there are around 20"; 4,500 kg at Delina; 200 kg typical; Wazpora; his son's restaurant. States the 14th-century arrival as fact.
- **onmanorama** — IANS, "Wazwan: The royal meal of paradise", Onmanorama, 10 November 2021. Rogan josh "Shoulder meat cooked in Praan paste, mawal flower essence, curd, and Kashmiri mirchi diluted essence"; "Rogan means fat and Josh means to cook on high heat"; marchwagun korma "three-quarters of the chilly used in the entire wazwan is singularly used in this dish"; aab gosht sheep ribs in milk with cardamoms and saffron; daniwal korma coriander, ghee, yogurt gravy; goshtaba "cheesecake-like texture", gravy of curd, cardamom, dry ginger and fennel; tabakh maaz ribs cooked in ghee then simmered in yogurt till dry; meat "minced with a walnut wood hammer on stone until it loses its stringiness"; 3 am start; wasta waza; meat cooked within one hour of butchering; nickel-plated copper on fruit-tree logs; flavour from flowers and herbs, via flavoured water or smoke; 21 mutton dishes; "easily 2 kg of mutton and chicken ... per person"; radish chutney between heavy dishes. States the Timur story as fact.
- **deccan-chronicle** — Namita Gupta, "Wazwan from the valley", *Deccan Chronicle*, 2 June 2016. Chef Bashir Ahmad Choncha (Choncha Foods, Srinagar): gushtaba "a heavy yogurt-based delicacy, where we pound the meat by hand for a very long time", "the king of all Kashmiri dishes", the grand finale; tabak maaz deep-fried in ghee, "It is important to use the chest for the required softness and taste"; "For Rogan josh neck and shoulders of the meat are preferable."
- **kashmir-life-2021** — "Kashmir Wazwan", *Kashmir Life*, 21 January 2021. History from the *Tuhfat-ul-Ahbab*: 16th-century feasts with kabab, pulao and yakhni; cooks hired for weddings "a guess" as forerunners of wazas; the copper *tabak* "maybe a forerunner of traem"; the author's view that wazwan took shape by the late Afghan period; "Abi Ghost" rich, originating as "poor man's food" in Iran; a caption calling gushtaba "the finest delicacy of Wazwaan" with the folklore of Bakhshi feeding gushtaba to a visiting Soviet leader.
- **kashmir-life-2011** — Abdul Mohamin, "Inalienable ingredient", *Kashmir Life*, 6 June 2011. Bashir Ahmad Khan, master chef, Fateh Kadal: "In wazwan hurry does not achieve the best curry"; not averse to food-grade colours if other ingredients are unadulterated; "It is not only the quality of the mutton that decides the taste." Cuts: rogan josh and korma from the bony portions; back leg for gushtaba and kebabs; ribs for tabakhmaz. Rista and gushtaba pounded by "a line of chefs using wooden mallets on stone slabs". Families still in the trade: Bandare, Khosas, Shaitan; Wazapora named after the wazas. Prof Fayaz (history): meat eaten since the Hindu period; no wazwan-like cuisine in the *Nilmat Puran* or *Rajatarangini*; experts who searched Central Asia "could not find these there". Pay per quintal.
- **anadolu-2022** — Iftikhar Gilani, "Winning hearts and minds through famed Kashmiri cuisine", Anadolu Agency, 21 June 2022. Tabakh maaz ribs cooked in milk then fried in clarified butter; gushtaba a large white meatball, last meat dish before sweets; 10 to 36 dishes overnight in *daegs*; a 1974 government order limiting hosts to five dishes was set aside by sessions judge Ghulam Nabi Gowhar, who said it would waste meat and recommended limiting guests; claims "waz" from Sanskrit *waja* and "waza" from Persian *aashpaz* (both unsupported here).
- **daily-excelsior-2023** — M. Saleem Beg (former Director General of Tourism, Convener INTACH J&K), "Cuisines of Kashmir: A tradition and a treasure trove", *Daily Excelsior*, 5 February 2023. "7 standard dishes that are must for Wazawan"; "Pandit cuisine uses dried ginger and asafoetida ... generously, while Muslim cookery uses onions and garlic"; shallot with garlic by Muslims; roganjosh in Pandit cookery "a spicy gravy with lamb chunks"; kabargah/tabakmaz "the method of preparation remains exactly the same", "seems to have travelled from Kazakhstan" (unsupported); gushtaba "pounded mutton balls cooked in preheated curd ... the final dish"; cooking "receives heat on two sides, top and bottom", "best results ... by slow heat using charcoal"; trami with a "hoof of boiled rice", sarposh; wazas father to son, claiming Samarkand descent.
- **dawn-2014** — Bisma Tirmizi, "Food Stories: Roghan Josh", *Dawn*, 9 December 2014. Quotes Lizzie Collingham (*Curry: A Tale of Cooks and Conquerors*): "Rogan Josh originated in Persia"; the Mughals' presence "encouraged a blossoming of Kashmiri cuisine"; the dish "was perfected" in Kashmir; Mughals cultivated saffron and asafoetida; Brahmans "avoid onions and garlic" and use fennel seeds and asafoetida; the Muslim version "lots of garlic and onion" and "the dried flower of the cockscomb plant (maval)". "Some food historians" link the name to red, roghan meaning red in Kashmiri. Frying asafoetida "released a garlicky flavour". "Among the seven permanent dishes in wazwan."
- **vir-sanghvi** — Vir Sanghvi, "Rogan Josh is India's greatest mutton curry", virsanghvi.com (undated; comments 2014 to 2015). "Came to India with the Mughals (we think) perhaps from Persia"; "really found favour only in Kashmir"; "It is the Kashmiris who created the version we know today." Virender Razdan (ITC, grew up in Srinagar) and Deepak Haksar (ITC, Kashmiri family): Pandits use saunth, aniseed, hing and dry spices; garam masala sometimes at the end; colour from Kashmiri chillies and the browning of the yoghurt; Muslims use onion and garlic and extract colour from mawal; Mehrotra's notes: the Muslim version has ghee or animal fat; Sandeep Bamzai: the two versions "completely different, not at all alike"; Vir: "still recognisably the same dish". Cuts: "more prosperous families used better cuts", "the whole machhli, the most tender part of the shoulder" (Haksar); Razdan "never had" the lamb-shank version growing up; "perhaps the cut you use is only a function of what you can afford". Haksar: enjoy it "with rice, not with a roti or a naan". The Kwality-Volga "dubious variation"; England's curry houses; "The Punjabi restaurant variation is based on the Hindu recipe."
- **outlook-traveller-2017** — Benny Joshua (the page's byline; an earlier version of this register said Rimli Sengupta, who does not appear on the page), "Kashmir's Legendary Wazwan Cuisine", Outlook Traveller, 28 July 2017. A 600-guest, 12-course wedding near Amira Kadal; 25 men; eight pounders each with a stone mortar and heavy wooden mallet, each adding a spice, the last pounding in lamb fat "turning the mash pink"; brass vats on a trench fire, heat regulated by moving vats. Glossary: rogan josh "mutton slow cooked in ghee and its own juices, generously spiced with Kashmiri chillies and flavoured with ginger"; daniwal korma "tender meat on the bone, in a white gravy flavoured with coriander"; marchwangan korma "mutton in a gravy red with Kashmiri chillies, flavoured with cardamom and coriander"; tabak maaz "unspiced rib cuts fried to a crisp; a sister dish of qabargah"; gushtaba "the last and most prized serving"; aab gosht "mutton in milk sauce"; wazwan "up to 36 courses", minimum seven.
- **jk-arts-2024** — Arka Chakraborty, "Kashmiri Wazwan: Celebration of Food, Culture and People", JK Arts Foundation, 13 August 2024. Cooking starts around 3 am; meat slaughtered hours before; beaten "till the nerves dissolve"; nickel-plated copper on dried wood; wazas speak "a code language"; vasta waza supervises; booking months ahead; tream/trami on white sheets over carpets; tasht-e-naer; radish chutney between heavy dishes; seven to 36 dishes; the 2017 law (seven and seven); "no documentary evidence" for the origin; Timur and Zain-ul-Abidin accounts presented as unverified; gushtaba "a curd-based creamy lamb meat curry", last.
- **sbs-2021** — Afnan Malik and Paras Nagpal, SBS Urdu, 9 November 2021. "Yousta Waza" supervising up to 10 apprentices; goshtaba pounded with a wooden hammer, simmered for hours; fresh slaughter key to the spongy texture; 14 to 36 dishes; Sandeep Pandit's Samarkand account as his own.
- **wire-2018** — Mudasir Ahmad, "From Poland to Kashmir, With Love", *The Wire*, 25 July 2018. Tabakhmaaz "pieces of lamb ribs fried in butter"; the traem; a Polish bride's favourite. Nothing on origins, despite Wikipedia citing it for one.
- **chef-jolly** — "Culinary Expedition to Kashmir", chefjolly.com, undated (c. 2021), a chef's travel notes. Gulam Nabi, waza at Gulmarg "for over 30 odd years"; Waza Bakshi, "Amma Kaka". Rogan josh cut "from the shoulder, saddle and ribs", mawal flower stock; rista red from chilli, with praan, garlic, red onion and chilli extract; both meatballs poached in stock with cinnamon and black cardamom; tabak maaz ribcage boiled two hours in stock with turmeric, fennel seeds, cardamom, cinnamon and salt, shallow-fried under a weight; "pundits boil the ribs in milk, whereas stock is preferred by Muslims"; aab gosh milk reduced with cinnamon, green cardamom, praan, garlic, ginger and fennel powders; dhaniwal korma praan and garlic roasted, fresh coriander, blanched almonds optional; marchwangan korma "sharpness due to the chili extract", pot-roasted. A personal blog; attribute explicitly and use sparingly.
- **rising-kashmir-2023** — Kainaat Syed, "Recipe: Kashmiri Aab Gosh (Dodhe Maaz)", *Rising Kashmir*, 16 April 2023. Tail, shoulder or shank; 500 g full-cream dry milk; fennel, ginger powder, cloves, cardamoms, cinnamon, garlic, fried shallot paste, ghee; almonds to garnish.
- **jktdc** — J&K Tourism Development Corporation, "Cuisines of Kashmir". 36 courses, 15 to 30 of meat; the wazas "claim to be the descendants" of cooks from Samarkand; vasta waza; no cutlery; Pandit kitchen: asafoetida and curd, originally no onion or garlic, young goat preferred, cuts from legs, neck, breast, ribs and shoulders, curd in almost every dish.
- **budgam** — District Budgam, "Wazwan". Mirrors Wikipedia's text on the traem and the dishes (including the chicken marchhwangan korma line); waz = cook, wan = shop.
- **gi-1336, gi-1337** — the Geographical Indications Registry records for Tabakmaaz and Goshtaba, as in the GI guide.
