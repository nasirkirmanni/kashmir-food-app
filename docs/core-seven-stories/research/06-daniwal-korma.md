## What it is, and its standing

- Body: "lamb roasted with yoghurt, spices and onion puree, topped with coriander leaves" (uncited); list entry "Daniwal korme (a mutton curry with coriander)" (uncited) — wp-wazwan; budgam.
- "Danival kormeh: lamb cooked with coriander or parsley" (uncited) — wp-list.
- Stub: lamb or mutton in a yogurt-based gravy flavoured with fresh coriander leaves; "a staple of the Kashmiri Wazwan" (cited to *The New Indian Express* 2024, Slurrp, NDTV's Waza Brothers recipe); "daniwal" means coriander (Times Now 2025); "korme" or "korma" means braise (Merriam-Webster) — wp-daniwal.
- One of the seven "a must" — wp-wazwan; "dhaniwal korma" among the seven "indispensable" — roli; "dhania kurma" among the seven standard dishes — daily-excelsior-2023.
- "mutton cooked in a lot of coriander" — outlook-diplomacy. "Daniwal Korma (mutton cooked in coriander)" — gulf-news-2022. "Mutton Korma cooked in coriander and Ghee with yogurt-based gravy" — onmanorama. "superbly tender meat on the bone, in a white gravy flavoured with coriander" — outlook-traveller-2017 glossary.

## The name

- *dāñĕwal*, p. 232: greens of the leaves of the coriander plant; *-gūjü*, powdered and sifted coriander seed; *-kulu*, the coriander shrub — grierson (search "coriander").
- Korma, from *qormā*, braising: meat or vegetables braised with yogurt, water or stock; lamb and chicken kormas common in the Wazwan — wp-korma (site's registry).
- "korma has Turkish roots" (Rather et al. 2016) — wp-kashmiri-cuisine.
- Shams-ud-din Ahmad's list of dishes Persian chefs taught includes korma — kashmir-walla.
- A search of Grierson's dictionary returns no entry for korma — grierson.

## How it is made

- Blanch the lamb cuts and remove the scum; "lightly roast both praan and garlic until golden, then add a little stock"; finish with the cooked lamb, fresh coriander, spices and blanched almonds (optional) — chef-jolly.
- Yogurt gravy with plenty of fresh coriander — wp-daniwal; outlook-diplomacy. Coriander, ghee, yogurt-based gravy — onmanorama.
- Rogan josh and korma made from the bony portions of the lamb — kashmir-life-2011.
- None of the sources mentions chilli; only Outlook Traveller describes the colour ("white gravy") — site's dish notes, confirmed.
- Chicken version: "daniwal korma kokar, chicken prepared with coriander", among the dishes added at hosts' demands (Khan) — kashmir-walla. Lamb and chicken kormas common in the Wazwan — wp-korma.

## Its place in the feast

- After rogan josh, before marchhwangan korma — outlook-diplomacy. In the mid-meal run with sabz haakh, rogan josh, marchwagun korma and doon chetin — gulf-news-2022.

## Deep pass, 15 October 2026 (expansion from 7 to 20 minutes)

### The name (additions)
- Hindi-Urdu dhania for leaf and seed; Kashmiri suffixes — our reading of Grierson's *dāñĕwal* / *-gūjü* / *-kulu* entries and the Times Now gloss cited by wp-daniwal. "anglicisation of the Hindi-Urdu qormā"; "Turkic qawirma, '[a] fried thing'"; "quite different dishes" — wp-korma.

### The herb
- Mediterranean origin; Israeli finds; Tutankhamen's tomb (~500 mL mericarps; "does not grow wild in Egypt"); Ebers Papyrus; Linear B at Pylos "cultivated for the manufacture of perfumes"; manna; coriandre/coriandrum; kóris, "on account of its fetid, bug-like smell"; "fresh, slightly citrus taste"; 2-decenal, 2-dodecenal; linalool, pinene; OR6A2; seeds in garam masala; leaves "used in large amounts and cooked until the flavor diminishes" — wp-coriander. "The only dish on the trami whose main flavour a share of the guests are born unable to enjoy" is ours, resting on OR6A2.

### A cool-season plant at an autumn feast
- Myers: "Both temperature and day length influence flowering and seed set."; "bolts into small white flowers and produces seed within four to six weeks"; "for several weeks or months before flowering"; early spring and late summer; "sow seeds in succession" — osu-cilantro. "Coriander (danival) is a cool herb whose season predominates between spring and summer in cooler areas." — wp-kashmiri-cuisine (Kashmir Life). Autumn wedding season — jms-skims-2012 ("when most of marriages in Kashmir take place"); reuters-2026 ("as the wedding season opens in autumn"). Foods "classed as hot or cold and combined with care"; dried vegetables important in winter (p. 254) — lawrence-1895. Where autumn coriander comes from: stated as unrecorded. The hot/cold placement: stated as not found in any waza's words.
- Hokh syun list (dried tomatoes, fenugreek, lotus stem, knapweed, shallot leaves, mint, gourd, turnip, quince, greens, spinach); "peeled, chopped, salted and sun-dried" — wp-kashmiri-cuisine. Coriander absent from the list: our observation, hedged.
- No "coriander", "dhania", "chillies" in Lawrence; food chapter contents (p. 253) — lawrence-1895 (full-text search).

### The base: praan and the stock

"the life of Wazwan" — asia-media-centre. *prān* 2 "fried onions (as a seasoning)"; *ganḍ*; no shallot headword — grierson.
- Frying rule ("Whole spices must be tempered ... Fried too little ... A second too much ..."; "Hot ghee or mustard oil is poured on top and garnished with fried praan (shallots) paste & saffron extract") — wp-kashmiri-cuisine (Guardian; Jolly). *bagāra* — grierson.
