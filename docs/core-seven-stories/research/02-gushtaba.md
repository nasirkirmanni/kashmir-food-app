## The name and the titles

- Grierson, *gōshĕth*, p. 309: "meat, flesh"; sub-entry "--āba or gusht-āba m. a rich soup of pounded or minced meat (K.Pr. 208)" — grierson (search "gosht").
- *Āb* is water and *gōsht* meat in the Persian name abgoosht — wp-abgoosht (already in the site's registry, used on the aab gosht page).
- Grierson, *maʦh* 3, p. 609: "a soup containing balls (or cakes) of this pounded meat fried in oil" — grierson (a different dish, fried; shows pounded-meat balls in the language).
- Kofta is a loanword from Persian *koofta*, "pounded meat" — wp-kofta (cited to Ayto 1994 and the OED).
- Names of rogan josh, yakhaen, ab gosht, riste and goshtabh "stem from Persian sources" (Rather et al. 2016) — wp-kashmiri-cuisine.
- Spellings: Goshtaab (Wikipedia's title), goshtaba, gushtaba, Goashtaba (Kaul Kilam), "Goshtaba" on the GI register — wp-goshtaab; kaul-kilam; gi-1337.
- "The King of Dishes" or "Dish of Kings", what Muslims call it — kaul-kilam recipe 205. "king of Kashmiri wazwan" — wp-goshtaab. "It's considered the king of all Kashmiri dishes" (Bashir Ahmad Choncha) — deccan-chronicle. "the finest delicacy of Wazwaan" — kashmir-life-2021 caption. "the showstopper" (Mohammad Shafi Khan) — kashmir-walla. "a yardstick to measure the chef's culinary skills", "serves as a full stop" — gulf-news-2022. "the last and most prized serving" — outlook-traveller-2017. "the most eagerly awaited dish served at the end" — kashmir-life-2011.

## Origin

- "believed to have been introduced" during the Mughal emperors' reign; cuisine influenced by Central Asian, Persian and Mughal cooking (Sen 2015; *The Wire* 2018, which in fact says nothing about origins) — wp-goshtaab.
- Shams-ud-din Ahmad's book: Persian chefs taught kabab, roganjosh, korma, yakhni, pulao, aabgosh, kofta and rista — kashmir-walla (goshtaba not named; kofta and rista are).
- The *Tuhfat-ul-Ahbab*'s 16th-century feasts had kabab, pulao and yakhni, "meat cooked in curd" — kashmir-life-2021.
- Grierson, *yĕkhañĕ*, p. 1192: "a kind of dish (consisting of meat cooked with spices in curdled milk)" — grierson.
- No documentary evidence for Wazwan's origin — jk-arts-2024. "Traditional knowledge is poorly documented"; goshtaba among the key ethnic meat products — rather-2016 abstract.
- Application 1337, "Goshtaba (Meat Products) of Kashmir", filed 8 August 2024 by The Kashmir Wazwan Cooperative Limited, Chatterhama; "Pre Examination" on 15 October 2026 — gi-1337 (as recorded in the site's GI guide).

## The meat and the pounding

- Minced directly after slaughter with a wooden hammer on a flat stone; fresh sheep fat and black cardamom; shaped into larger balls than rista; boiled — sahapedia. Fresh sheep fat worked in until the paste turns light pink, then black cardamom seeds and salt (site's dish notes, from Sahapedia).
- "If the mutton is left idle and minced later, one can't make goshtab" (Khan) — kashmir-walla.
- Slaughtered fresh and pounded before rigor mortis sets; folded while beaten to make it light and fluffy; both meatball dishes rarely found outside the valley because the texture is hard to achieve — wp-kashmiri-cuisine (citing *The Hindu* 2018 and Chef Jolly).
- Meat prepared for cooking within one hour of butchering; "minced with a walnut wood hammer on stone until it loses its stringiness"; "cheesecake-like texture minced with fat and dipped in mutton broth" — onmanorama.
- Pounded "with Kashmiri walnut wood hammers on stone" until creamy; "a huge ball of meat, marinated with blobs of golden fat"; "a golden soup too, which is tingling" — gulf-news-2022.
- "the rhythmic thud of wooden mallets pounding lamb"; wazas "pound meat with wooden mallets on stone slabs to prepare gushtaba and rista"; still pounded by hand — reuters-2026.
- "a line of chefs using wooden mallets on stone slabs"; the back leg goes into gushtaba and kebabs — kashmir-life-2011.
- Eight men, each with a stone mortar and a heavy wooden mallet, each pounding a spice in and passing the meat on; the last pounds in lamb fat, "turning the mash pink"; "glistening oval discs"; in the gravy the melting fat stretched them into "enormous orbs" — outlook-traveller-2017.
- Beaten "till the nerves dissolve" — jk-arts-2024. Apprentices pound to the desired texture — asia-media-centre.
- "we pound the meat by hand for a very long time" (Choncha) — deccan-chronicle; "must be pounded for hours" — wp-goshtaab.
- Pounded with a wooden hammer; simmered for hours; freshly slaughtered meat key to the spongy texture — sbs-2021.
- Kaul Kilam recipe 190: boneless leg "preferably freshly slaughtered"; about 250 g suet softened in hot water; a wooden mallet (*goshpar*) on a flat hard stone; cubes pounded a few at a time, suet pounded in; ginger, aniseed, black cardamom, garam masala, salt and arrowroot kneaded in; a dough-like mass; balls 2 to 3 inches for goashtaba, smaller for rista; a food processor for small batches — kaul-kilam.
- Home version with egg whites, fat and mustard oil in the mince (Abdul Rashid Waza, "a Dubai-based Kashmiri homecook") — gulf-news-goshtaba. Riste with eggs (NDTV) — wp-riste.
- Meat science (industrial, not Kashmiri): as rigor sets in, falling pH cancels protein charges and actomyosin "physically reduces the space between protein chains", lowering water-holding capacity; pre-rigor muscle is "the easiest form, from which to extract the highly functional protein, myosin"; salt is mixed in "to preserve the pre-rigor state"; the result is "a firmer texture to the final cooked sausage, with less cooking loss"; for pork, salt within 90 minutes of bleeding — Lynn Knipe, Ohio State University, "Pre-Rigor / Hot Boning of Pork Carcasses", 26 April 2002 (meatsci.osu.edu/node/127). Hot-boned pre-rigor muscle has "excellent processing characteristics with high pH and relaxed microstructure"; salt improves protein solubility, emulsifying capacity and water-holding capacity and inactivates glycolytic enzymes — Kim, Yong, Jung, Kim and Choi, "Technologies for the Production of Meat Products with a Low Sodium Chloride Content and Improved Quality Characteristics—A Review", *Foods* 10(5), 957, 2021.

## The yakhni

- Yogurt and mutton broth with shallots, ginger, garlic, cinnamon and cardamom; topped with cumin and dry mint; "creamy white soup" — sahapedia.
- Curd, cardamom, dry ginger and fennel — onmanorama. Yoghurt, ghee and stock with mild spices and dry mint — kashmir-walla. "pounded mutton balls cooked in preheated curd" — daily-excelsior-2023. "a large white meatball served in yogurt with special spices" — anadolu-2022. "velvety textured meatball in white yogurt gravy"; "spicy yoghurt gravy" — wp-wazwan. Yakhni without turmeric or chilli powder — wp-list.
- Kaul Kilam recipe 205: balls simmered about half an hour with salt, aniseed and ginger powders; the broth strained and combined with curd; cooked with oil, ghee, cloves and garlic, stirred constantly so the curd doesn't split; fried shallot and onion; low heat until the oil and ghee rise; garam masala, cinnamon, black and green cardamom and mint to finish; served hot with a ladle of gravy — kaul-kilam.
- Home version: a spice pouch of ginger, fennel, cinnamon and cardamoms boiled with bay leaves; yogurt and milk; dried mint; saffron — gulf-news-goshtaba.
- Boiled in water until soft and expanded before the gravy (site's notes, from Sahapedia).

## The last dish

- "As a rule, the meal must end with gushtaba" — asia-media-centre.
- The main course "usually ends with gushtaba" (citing *Deccan Chronicle*); desserts follow — wp-wazwan.
- "the final dish of a Wazwan feast, signalling the end of the meal" — reuters-2026.
- "the final dish announcing the completion of servings" — daily-excelsior-2023.
- Yakhni "the finishing dish served in wazwan before gushtaba is served" — outlook-diplomacy.
- "the last Meat Dish in a 'Vazavan' Feast, before the Dessert" — kaul-kilam.
- One gushtaba of 200 to 400 g serves the four at a trami; the eldest portions it; kahwa closes the feast; one rista per person — sahapedia.
- One large meatball cut into four — kashmir-walla.
- Fresh rice often eaten just before it; its serving "indicates the end of the meal" — jk-arts-2024.
- Kehwa from a samovar after — gulf-news-2022. Final meat dish before the sweets — anadolu-2022.
- Kashmiris consider it disrespectful to refuse a dish or an extra helping — onmanorama (site's notes).
- None of these sources says why it comes last (the site's notes already say so).

## Deep pass, 15 October 2026 (expansion from 16 to 27 minutes)

### The cook, the kitchen and the feast, in the dictionary
- *wāza* "a cook-person, one who is a cook by profession, and comes of a line, or caste, of cooks"; *wāzagī* the profession; *nözir* and *sŏlhöri* head cook; *kömü* proverb "what have cooks to do with kings"; *wura* temporary feast kitchen; *wura-bal* "the place for the cooking-fires of a bridegroom's party"; *dēg* "a large pile of rice cooked in a cauldron, for a wedding feast or the like"; *dah* "materials (flesh, spices, etc.) for a great feast"; *yēra* "a marriage feast"; *trömi* "a copper dish (used only by Musalmāns) out of which food is eaten"; *trāmi-bata*; *pötar* "eating from the same dish or at the same feast"; *māzarath* "hospitable pressing to eat (at a feast)"; *zang* "a dish of curds" as a good omen — grierson (new entries). Dictionary dates (compiled from the 1890s, published 1916 to 1932) — Digital South Asia Library's description of the work. The zang-and-white-dish link is marked as unrecorded.

### The feast in 1895
- "when the professional cook is called in some taste is displayed in the preparation and arrangement of viands"; "the earthenware platters filled with brightly-coloured foods make a pretty show" (p. 253); mutton "perhaps five times in the month"; "Clarified butter (ghi) is not much eaten"; "the Kashmiri Musalman is extravagant and greedy" (p. 254); "the gorgeous groom sits on cushions and the feast commences, winding up with tea"; "a big feast is given on the marriage night" (p. 269); "frugal in their expenditure and do not lavish money on marriage feasts" (p. 397) — lawrence-1895. The earthenware-versus-copper observation is ours.

### Where it may have come from (additions)

### The meat (additions)
- The mallet's three names: *goshpar* — kaul-kilam recipe 190; "minced on stony surfaces with a tukni (a wooden instrument)" — jms-skims-2012; wooden mallets on stone slabs — reuters-2026.

### What the laboratory made of it
- Samoon 1988 M.V.Sc. thesis (IVRI) as the standard method — jalal-2015 (reference list and text).
- "Incorporation of lamb fat at 20% level was found optimum"; pre-rigor and chilled meat; hand-minced hot-processed product bound better than machine-minced cold-boned — samoon-sharma-1994 abstract.
- "Goshtaba forms the essential item of Kashmiri wazwan"; "an emulsion type ground meat product"; "Considerable amount of animal fat (20 %)"; hind-leg mutton; 2.5 % salt, large cardamom, cumin; yakhni two parts curd to one water with cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, ginger, aniseed, garlic, fried onion, salt; 30 min; balls 60 mm, 50 to 60 g; control 63.96 % moisture, 15.40 % protein, 17.03 % fat, pH 5.28, 7.3/9 — rather-2015-xanthan.
- "within 2 h of post-mortem"; 90:10 mutton to fat, 2.5 % salt, 10 % chilled water, 0.2 % large cardamom; "average fat content of 15–25% is having a high sensory appeal"; 20 days at 4 °C — jalal-2015.
- "None of the papers claims to have matched the wedding kitchen's ball" is our summary of the papers read; apple pomace and retort pouches are from the Rather group's other titles (seen in reference lists and Europe PMC; not read).
