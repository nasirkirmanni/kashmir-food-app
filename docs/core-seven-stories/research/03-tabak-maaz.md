# Tabak maaz: fact ledger

_Every claim the article makes, with its source. Source ids are in `sources.md`._

## What it is, and its standing

- "twice-cooked lamb ribs, initially braised with ground spices, then browned in ghee"; the traem arrives with "two tabakh maaz"; list entry "ribs of lamb simmered in yogurt till tender, then fried" (cited to *The Hindu* 2012) — wp-wazwan. Budgam: "braised with ground spices and milk, then browned in butter"; two tabak maaz on the traem — budgam.
- One of the seven "a must" — wp-wazwan; "indispensable" — roli; "standard dishes that are must" — daily-excelsior-2023; among the key ethnic meat products — rather-2016 abstract.
- Application 1336, "Tabakmaaz (Meat Products) of Kashmir", filed 8 August 2024 by The Kashmir Wazwan Cooperative Limited; "Pre Examination" on 15 October 2026 — gi-1336 (site's GI guide).

## The name

- *tabakh*, p. 962: a large dish, plate or tray; *māz*, p. 615: meat — grierson (site's registry).
- Kashmiri words for ribs: *pōwaca*, p. 810, "the ribs (of man or beast)"; *pāha-lara*, p. 684, "the ribs (spoken of as a whole)"; *lar* 2, p. 529, "a bone of the side, a rib" — grierson (search "rib").
- The 16th-century feasts of the *Tuhfat-ul-Ahbab*: food served on a dastarkhwan in a copper *tabak*, "maybe a forerunner of traem of today"; in grand ceremonies the tabak "covered with sarposh" — kashmir-life-2021.
- Kaul Kilam recipe 44, "Kabargah Ta Tabaq Maz": for tabaq maz the battered ribs are placed between ghee-smeared baked clay plates (*tabaqs*), the rims sealed with dough, cooked over slow charcoal with coals on top for about an hour; the earthenware gives a special flavour; "should always be served steaming hot" — kaul-kilam.
- "seems to have travelled from Kazakhstan", with nothing offered in support — daily-excelsior-2023.
- Spellings: tabak maaz (site), tabakh maaz (Wikipedia, Anadolu, *The Wire*), tabaq maz (Kaul Kilam), tabakmaz (Beg), Tabakmaaz (the GI register), qabargah/kabargah (the Pandit name) — various.

## The first cooking: what the ribs are simmered in

- Water, the Waza family's method: the whole rib cage boiled, skimmed "until the water runs clear", garlic then salt, boiled covered until the membrane can be pierced with a thumb; drained, cooled, washed; cut with a heavy knife into eight rectangular pieces; boiled again in the reserved water with salt, dry ginger, cloves, black cardamom and turmeric "until the bones can be extracted from the membrane easily" — scroll-tabakh-maaz (Roli).
- Whole ribs boiled with salt and garlic, chopped, fried — kashmir-walla. "boiled with salt and garlic" — wp-list.
- Fatty ribs simmered with cardamom, cinnamon, ginger powder, garlic paste and cloves, then cut and fried in ghee until crisp and caramelised; a starter — sahapedia (spice list as recorded in the site's dish notes, checked 12 October 2026).
- Stock, two hours, with turmeric, fennel seeds, cardamom, cinnamon and salt; "pundits boil the ribs in milk, whereas stock is preferred by Muslims" — chef-jolly.
- Milk: "simmered in milk and spices and then fried" — wp-tabak-maaz; "cooked in milk, then fried in clarified butter" — anadolu-2022; "braised with ground spices and milk" — budgam.
- Yogurt: "simmered in yogurt till tender, then fried" (*The Hindu*) — wp-wazwan list; "cooked in ghee then simmered in yogurt till dry" — onmanorama.
- Water with half a teacup of milk, the dry spices, cloves, salt and asafoetida, simmered until tender and the liquid absorbed — kaul-kilam recipe 44.
- Ribs in roughly 2 × 4 inch pieces with the fatty layer and skin on, two or three bones per piece, 25 to 30 pieces per kilo — kaul-kilam recipe 44.
- "It is important to use the chest for the required softness and taste" (Choncha) — deccan-chronicle. The ribs go to tabakhmaz — kashmir-life-2011. At a wedding "ribs were hacked into shape" — outlook-traveller-2017.

## The second cooking: ghee

- 475 g pure ghee to 1 kg of ribs; arranged without overlapping, ghee poured over, "fry until they are reddish brown all over", turning occasionally; "drain out the pure ghee before serving" — scroll-tabakh-maaz.
- "deep fried in ghee and is a very rich dish" (Choncha) — deccan-chronicle; "Tabak Maaz (deep fried ribs)" — gulf-news-2022.
- Shallow-fried "bone side down, using a weight on top to press it" — chef-jolly.
- "a glossy meat made of lamb ribs, fried thoroughly and served in dry form" — outlook-diplomacy. "unspiced rib cuts fried to a crisp; a sister dish of qabargah" — outlook-traveller-2017. "ribs cooked in ghee" — jk-arts-2024. "pieces of lamb ribs fried in butter" — wire-2018. "fried ribs" — reuters-2026.
- Kabargah: dipped in a curd batter (curd beaten with chilli powder, garam masala and salt) and deep-fried slowly in about 250 g ghee until brown — kaul-kilam recipe 44.

## Tabak maaz and kabargah

- Kashmiri Pandits commonly call it kabargah; kabargah "simmered on a low heat for longer and then fried very quickly", tabak maaz boiled with salt and garlic, slightly elastic — wp-list (citing Daily Excelsior and a Hyderabad food blog). Also called qabargah — wp-tabak-maaz.
- "The method of preparation remains exactly the same for this dish"; "Soft ribs from mutton"; Muslim name tabakmaz, Pandit name kabargah — daily-excelsior-2023.
- Kabargah among the delicacies of Kashmiri Pandit cooking — jktdc.
- Kaul Kilam: the two names are two finishes of one simmered rib; tabaq maz differs from kabargah "in its final cooking stage"; "generally served as a last Meat Course by Kashmiri Pandits" — kaul-kilam recipe 44.
- The Roli headnote contrasts the Muslim wazwaan with the Pandits' *butta*, both favouring lamb and wood fire — scroll-tabakh-maaz.
